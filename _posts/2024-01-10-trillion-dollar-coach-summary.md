I recently finished reading *The Trillion Dollar Coach* by Eric Schmidt, Jonathan Rosenberg, and Alan Eagle, and I have to say—this book is a gem. If you're managing people, you need to read this.

The book shares the leadership principles of Bill Campbell, who coached some of Silicon Valley's most successful leaders, including Steve Jobs, Larry Page, and Eric Schmidt. What struck me was how simple and human these principles are. They're not complicated management theories—they're just good, honest ways to lead people.

After reading it, I found myself thinking: "Why isn't this how we all manage?" So I wanted to share the key principles that stuck with me.

## The Foundation: It's About People

**Your title makes you a manager. Your people make you a leader.**

This hit me hard. We've all had managers who hide behind their title, right? Bill's point was simple: leadership isn't given—it's earned. When your team trusts you and respects you, that's when you're actually leading.

**Build trust by actually caring.**

Not the fake "how was your weekend?" kind of caring. Real caring. Bill would remember details about people's families, their struggles, their goals. He'd check in not because it was on his to-do list, but because he genuinely wanted to know how they were doing.

**Be present. Like, really present.**

You know that meeting where someone's checking their phone the whole time? When you're with someone, be *with* them. Phone away. Laptop closed. Full attention. It sounds simple, but how many of us actually do this?

## How to Actually Talk to People

**Listen more than you talk.**

This one's harder than it sounds. Ask questions and then... wait. Don't jump in with solutions. Don't finish people's sentences. Just listen. And somehow, people will figure things out just by talking through them.

**Tell the truth, even when it's uncomfortable.**

Be direct. Not mean—just honest. If something isn't working, say so. No sugar-coating. But here's the thing: people respect it. They know where they stand. How many of us have worked in places where no one tells you what's really going on?

**Coach the person, not just fix the problem.**

When someone comes to you with a problem, don't just solve it. Help them think through it. Ask: "What do you think you should do?" Build people, not just fix things.

## Building Teams That Actually Work

**Only coach people who want to be coached.**

This was refreshing. Don't waste time trying to coach people who aren't open to feedback. Focus on those who want to grow. It's okay to say: "This person isn't coachable right now." You don't have to fix everyone.

**Build teams around people, not products.**

Great products come from great teams. Spend time getting the right people together, making sure they can work well with each other. Chemistry matters. You can't just throw smart people in a room and expect magic.

**Hold people accountable, but support them when things go wrong.**

Clear expectations. Follow through. But when someone messes up? Help them learn from it. Don't just point fingers. Be tough but fair, and let people know you have their back.

## Making Decisions

**Use data, but trust your gut.**

We live in a data-driven world, and that's good. But sometimes you have to make a call based on what feels right. Data informs, but your experience matters too.

**Don't let urgent stuff kill the important stuff.**

How many times have you spent a whole day putting out fires and realized you didn't do anything that actually mattered? Protect time for the big picture. Say no to things that don't align with what really matters.

## Being a Real Human Leader

**Be yourself.**

You don't have to be Steve Jobs or some other famous leader. Be you. Your authentic style is your strength. Don't try to copy someone else's style—your unique approach is what makes you effective.

**It's okay to not know everything.**

Admit when you don't have the answer. Say "I don't know, let's figure it out together." That vulnerability? It builds connection. People don't need you to be perfect. They need you to be real.

**Take care of yourself.**

You can't pour from an empty cup. Maintain your health, your relationships, your life outside work. Model that for the people you work with. You can't help others if you're burned out.

## Why This Matters

Here's what I love about these principles: they're not complicated. There's no fancy framework or 10-step process. It's just: care about people, tell them the truth, and help them succeed.

But here's the thing—it works. The leaders who followed these principles went on to build some of the most successful companies in the world. Not because of complex strategies, but because they learned to lead with humanity.

*The Trillion Dollar Coach* is one of those books that changes how you think about leadership. Bill Campbell's legacy lives on through the leaders he coached, and thanks to this book by Eric Schmidt, Jonathan Rosenberg, and Alan Eagle, his wisdom is available to all of us.

If you manage people—or want to—this book is worth your time. It's not about being perfect. It's about being human, and that's something we can all do.
